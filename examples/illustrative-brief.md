# Illustrative ABC research brief

**Status: provisional teaching example.** No researcher has confirmed this profile, no participants have been recruited, and no literature search has been run. This example demonstrates reasoning and output structure; it is not an evidence-grounded proposal ready for submission.

## Starting point and intended contribution

**Topic-led seed:** how first-year students decide whether to use peer feedback in one online design studio. No seed paper has yet been selected. The next coaching step is to propose credible seed candidates and let the researcher confirm one.

**Aim:** explain students’ decisions to accept, adapt, or disregard peer feedback during a bounded studio assignment.

**Annotation:** this aim seeks understanding of decisions. It does not also promise an evaluated intervention or a causal estimate of learning gains.

## ABC profile and questions

- **Belief:** subjective; students’ interpretations and the local context are central to the proposed account. This remains a choice for the researcher to confirm.
- **Concern:** concrete; the intended output explains decisions in a situated feedback process.
- **Quadrant:** Explain.
- **Output:** an interpretive explanation of feedback-use decisions, with cautiously stated implications for studio practice.
- **Q1:** How do first-year students in this online design studio decide which peer feedback to use when revising an assignment?
- **Q2:** Why do students accept, adapt, or disregard particular suggestions in that revision process?

**Alignment check:** Q1 examines the decision process; Q2 examines participants’ reasons and contextual influences. Together they can support a situated explanation. They cannot establish that peer feedback caused improved learning.

**Rationale:** the explanation could inform later intervention design. Designing and evaluating that intervention would be a separate Develop cycle.

## Evidence and feasible methods outline

| Question | Evidence needed | Candidate collection and analysis | Scope/access to confirm | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Feedback, successive drafts, and accounts of revision decisions | Compare feedback with revisions; use stimulated-recall interviews to build case accounts | One assignment cycle; permission to access drafts and recruit a feasible sample | Draft changes alone cannot reveal motivation; interviews are retrospective accounts |
| Q2 | Participants’ reasons, counterexamples, and contextual differences | Interpretive coding and comparison across contrasting cases | Recruitment, consent, privacy, ethics review, and researcher role | Accounts may be selective; one studio limits transferability; no causal effect estimate |

**Annotation:** these methods follow the evidence needed, not an automatic “Explain means interviews” rule. Sample size and recruitment remain feasibility decisions rather than invented commitments.

## Literature stress test: not yet executed

Proposed queries include “peer feedback uptake design education,” “student feedback literacy revision decisions,” and “online studio peer critique.” An initial search would select 8–12 relevant works, including competing explanations and method sources, with one bounded citation-expansion round.

The search log must record date, source, exact queries/filters, inspected access level, selection decisions, and DOI/OpenAlex identifiers. No sources or findings are invented here. Existing studies might require a different context, narrower construct, replication, or a revised contribution.
