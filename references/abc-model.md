The paper adapts Burrell and Morgan's paradigms, with reference to Roode, to help inexperienced researchers align aims and questions, especially in educational technology. It acknowledges criticism of paradigm exclusivity, including Deetz (1996). This is an initial focus generator, not a substitute for a fully justified philosophy or methodology.

## A, B, and C

- **Aim:** what this study is to accomplish: explore, explain, develop, or describe. Clarify the intended output rather than classifying by an isolated verb in a draft aim.
- **Belief:** assumptions about scientific knowledge/truth and technology. The model contrasts subjective, context-dependent understanding with objective, determinable answers or justified prescriptions. Cronjé associates these with anti-positivist and positivist positions. Do not treat numerical answers as automatically objective, or interviews as automatically subjective.
- **Concern:** the conceptualisation of the research in relation to society. Cronjé simplifies radical change/regulation into abstract/concrete: conceptual pictures of a situation versus concrete explanation, rules, or solutions. These equivalences belong to his adaptation. “Abstract” does not mean impractical; “concrete” does not mean atheoretical. Ask about the envisaged output and social purpose, not just whether the topic sounds theoretical.

Ask the researcher to describe the answer they hope to produce. A map of unfamiliar possibilities, an account of how and why a situation occurs, a workable intervention, and a model of components and their relationships imply different projects even when they concern the same technology.

## Canonical mappings (sections 3–4, Figure 3)

| Aim | Model paradigm | Belief | Concern | Questions | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explore | Radical humanist | Subjective | Abstract | What is/are…? + How does/do…? | Map/chart of possibilities and experiences |
| Explain | Interpretive | Subjective | Concrete | How does/do…? + Why is/does…? | Explanation, rules, heuristics, or algorithms |
| Develop | Functionalist | Objective | Concrete | Why is/does…? + How should…? | Solution |
| Describe | Radical structuralist | Objective | Abstract | What is/are…? + How should…? | Model |

The two axes provide the pair: subjective/objective selects “how does”/“how should”; abstract/concrete selects “what”/“why.” Ordering the pair does not change the quadrant. “Describe” includes relationships and composition of a model; it is not limited to a simple inventory. “Explain” in this model does not, by itself, establish a causal effect.

## Coaching examples (new illustrations, not quotations)

For a study of peer feedback in an online design studio:

- **Explore:** What opportunities and tensions arise when students exchange peer feedback? How do students experience and respond to them?
- **Explain:** How do students decide which peer feedback to use? Why do they accept some suggestions and disregard others?
- **Develop:** Why does the current feedback process fail to support revision? How should a feedback intervention be designed and evaluated to address the identified barriers?
- **Describe:** What components constitute a feedback-support model? How should those components relate to represent the process coherently?

Use examples to clarify options, not to replace the researcher's decisions. “How should” questions need stated criteria and evidence; they are not requests for the assistant's personal preference. A development question about failure must not assume failure that has not been established: phrase the premise as a reported concern or investigate whether/how barriers exist.

## Aim, outcome, and rationale (sections 4.1–4.2)

Aim = intended accomplishment. Outcome = what answering the questions produces. Rationale = why that output matters and what it could enable next. The model's cycle is:

**Describe → Explore → Explain → Develop → Describe.**

A description can enable exploration; exploration can support explanation; explanation can inform development; a solution can be described as a transferable model. Do not promise that the present study will accomplish the next phase merely because it provides the rationale.

## Multiple aims and caveats

Cronjé requires one quadrant at a time. If a researcher wants to understand experiences and then design an intervention, offer sequential cycles: each has its own aim, ABC profile, questions, evidence, and output, with a clear hand-off. Not every study needs the full cycle, and its starting point depends on prior evidence.

Preserve that logic within an ABC session, but attribute the exclusivity claim to this model. Do not assert that mixed methods, pragmatism, critical realism, or integrated research programmes are intrinsically incoherent. If a requested approach exceeds the framework, explain the mismatch and distinguish a researcher-authorised adaptation from the original ABC model.

The paper explicitly says associating paradigms with qualitative/quantitative methods is speculative (section 6.4). Method choice must follow the question, evidence, access, and warranted inference. It presents purposively selected cases, not universal validation of the model. Its social-science and educational-technology origins limit automatic generalisation to other fields.
