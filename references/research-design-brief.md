# Research-design brief

Use this structure flexibly; omit empty headings and adapt detail to the session. Mark the brief **confirmed** only when the researcher has confirmed the ABC profile and question pair. Otherwise label it **provisional**, naming the specific open choices.

## Starting point and contribution

Identify the seed with verified bibliographic metadata, DOI/link, and access level (full text, abstract, or metadata only). Summarise what it actually establishes. State the proposed new contribution and distinguish an author-reported limitation from an inferred opportunity. Give the setting/population and practical scope.

## ABC and questions

- **Aim:** one clear sentence describing what the study itself will accomplish.
- **Belief:** the model position and the researcher's reasons for it.
- **Concern:** abstract/concrete position and its social meaning in this project.
- **Quadrant and expected output:** the corresponding ABC aim and deliverable.
- **Q1 and Q2:** the focused question pair, with constructs and boundaries explained.
- **Alignment check:** explain how the two answers together achieve the aim.
- **Rationale:** explain why the output matters and the next activity it could enable; distinguish this from an additional aim.

For multiple cycles, repeat these items per cycle and describe which output informs the next.

## Evidence and methods outline

| Question | Evidence needed | Candidate collection/source | Candidate analysis | Feasible scope/access | Limits on inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Choose methods on evidential grounds. For example, interpretive explanation may need accounts and observations across contrasting cases; a development prescription may need requirements, an intervention, and evaluation against explicit criteria; a model may need document synthesis and validation. These are possibilities, not mandatory quadrant-to-method mappings. Identify realistic recruitment/data access, consequential consent or ethics needs, and outstanding feasibility decisions without inventing a full protocol.

## Literature check and next decisions

Provide a concise selection of closely relevant sources with title, author/year where verified, DOI or canonical link, access level, and a sentence on how each affects the question. Distinguish supportive, competing, prior-solution, and methodological roles by reading content, not citation edges.

State queries/filters, retrieval date, discovery sources, citation-expansion boundaries, selection decisions, and incomplete coverage. Use “not found within these searches” for a tentative absence. A search log can be a small table; do not include credentials. List unresolved decisions and a short practical reading/data-access next step. Do not describe an unavailable search or uninspected full text as completed.
